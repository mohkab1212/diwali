Why we celebrate

Deepavali is Sanskrit for a row of lamps. The festival falls on the new moon of the month of Kartik, the darkest night of the season, and all over India the answer is the same: light every lamp you own.

The story behind it changes with the region and the faith.

Ram comes home

Ram, prince of Ayodhya, spent 14 years in exile and fought Ravana, the king of Lanka, to bring his wife Sita back. He returned with Sita and his brother Lakshman on a moonless night. The people of Ayodhya lined the streets and rooftops with clay lamps to light the way home. This is the story most of north India tells.

Lakshmi visits

Lakshmi is the goddess of wealth and good fortune. She is said to have risen from the ocean when the gods and demons churned it for the nectar of immortality. On Diwali night she walks the earth and enters homes that are clean, lit and open. So we clean for a week, draw a rangoli at the door and leave the lights on.

Krishna defeats Narakasura

In the south and west, the day before Diwali marks Krishna's victory over the demon king Narakasura, who held thousands of women captive. Families wake before sunrise, bathe with oil and light lamps to mark the end of his rule.

Two more faiths, the same night

Jains mark the night Mahavira, the last of their 24 great teachers, attained nirvana. Sikhs celebrate Bandi Chhor Divas, the day Guru Hargobind was released from Gwalior Fort and brought 52 imprisoned kings out with him.

A new year and new books

For many trading families, Gujarati and Marwari among them, Diwali is the end of the business year. The old accounts are settled and a new ledger is opened in front of Lakshmi, a ritual called Chopda Pujan. The stock exchanges in Mumbai still open for a one-hour Muhurat trading session on Diwali.

In every version, light wins over darkness.

The five days

Diwali is five days long and each day has its own job. These are the dates for 2026.

  1. Day 1 · Friday 6 NovemberToday

    Dhanteras

    The day of wealth and health. People buy something new for the house: gold, silver, a steel pot, even a broom. In the evening a lamp goes at the front door, and one more faces south for Yama, the god of death, to keep him away for another year.

  2. Day 2 · Saturday 7 NovemberToday

    Choti Diwali

    Small Diwali, also called Naraka Chaturdashi. The rangoli is finished, the first few diyas go out and boxes of sweets start moving between houses. In the south and west the day begins with an oil bath before sunrise.

  3. Day 3 · Sunday 8 NovemberToday

    Diwali, the night of Lakshmi Puja

    The main night. New clothes, a puja for Lakshmi and Ganesh after sunset, then every lamp in the house is lit. After that come dinner, sweets, sparklers and a game of cards that runs late.

  4. Monday 9 November is a gap day this year. The new moon carries into Monday, so the fourth day moves to Tuesday.

  5. Day 4 · Tuesday 10 NovemberToday

    Govardhan Puja and Annakut

    Krishna lifted Govardhan hill on one finger to shelter his village from a storm sent by Indra. Temples build a mountain of food in thanks, called Annakut, often with 56 dishes. For Gujaratis this is new year's day, and the greeting is Saal Mubarak.

  6. Day 5 · Wednesday 11 NovemberToday

    Bhai Dooj

    The day for brothers and sisters. A sister puts a tilak on her brother's forehead and prays for his long life. He gives her a gift and promises to look after her. The story goes that Yama visited his sister Yamuna on this day.

Dates are for India and Australia. In the UK and North America the fourth and fifth days fall a day earlier, on 9 and 10 November. The lamps on the left light up as each day arrives.

Puja at home

You do not need a priest. A clean corner, a lamp and about 30 minutes are enough. Every family has its own order, so treat this as the simple version.

The eight steps

  1. Clean and open up

    Tidy the house and switch on the lights. Draw a rangoli at the front door. Many families add small footprints leading inside, for Lakshmi.

  2. Set the table

    Spread the red cloth. Place Ganesh and Lakshmi side by side, the kalash in front, and the sweets, fruit, flowers and coins around them.

  3. Light the first lamp

    Light one diya and say the lamp mantra. Every other lamp tonight is lit from this one.

  4. Start with Ganesh

    Ganesh removes obstacles, so every puja begins with him. Put a tilak of kumkum on the picture, offer a flower and a few grains of rice, and say his mantra.

  5. Welcome Lakshmi

    Do the same for Lakshmi: tilak, flowers, rice. Offer the sweets and the coins. Chant her mantra 11 times, or 108 if you have the time.

  6. Sing the aarti

    Everyone stands. One person circles the thali with a lit lamp in front of the deities, clockwise, while someone rings the bell and everyone sings Om Jai Jagdish Hare. Then the thali goes around the room. Pass both hands over the flame and bring them to your eyes.

  7. Prasad and blessings

    Close with the prayer for everyone's wellbeing. Then share the sweets that were offered. The young touch the feet of the elders and get a blessing, and usually an envelope.

  8. Light the house

    Carry diyas to every room, the doorstep, the windows and the balcony. Leave one burning through the night where it is safe, and leave the front door open for a while.

Get ready

A checklist for the week and ideas for the house. Your ticks stay on this phone.

0 done

Before the first match

  • Cotton clothes near the lamps. Tie back long hair, dupattas and loose sleeves.
  • Keep a bucket of water or sand within reach.
  • For a burn, hold it under cool running water for 20 minutes. No ice, butter or toothpaste. See a doctor if it blisters or is bigger than a 20 cent coin.
  • Put out every flame before you sleep or leave the house.
  • Fireworks at home need a licence in New South Wales, and most of Australia is the same. Check the rules where you live.

A line of diyas up the steps

Set clay diyas a hand apart along one side of the steps or the path to the door. Fill them with oil or ghee and keep the wick short, so the flame stays small and steady.

Keep it safe. Use the side people do not walk on. Saris, dupattas and children's sleeves catch at ankle height.

Floating lamps in a bowl

Fill a wide brass or glass bowl with water. Float marigold heads, rose petals and three or four floating candles. It works as a table centre or by the front door.

Keep it safe. Put it where it cannot be knocked, and keep it well away from fairy lights and power boards.

A marigold toran on the door

Thread marigolds on strong cotton with a needle, with a mango or ashoka leaf between every few flowers. Hang it across the top of the door frame.

Keep it safe. Hang it high enough to clear heads and well clear of any lamp below it.

Rangoli at the entrance

Chalk the outline first, then fill it with coloured powder, rice flour or flower petals. A simple eight-petal flower looks better than a complicated design done in a hurry. You can try a pattern in the rangoli corner first.

Keep it safe. Powder is slippery on tiles, so draw it to one side of the walkway. Stand any diya in the rangoli on a small plate.

A paper lantern in the window

An akash kandil, the star or lantern of coloured paper, goes in a window or on the balcony where the street can see it.

Keep it safe. Light it with an LED bulb. Never put a candle inside paper.

Fairy lights on the balcony and the door frame

Warm white along the railing and around the front door does most of the work. Add one colour if you want more, not five.

Keep it safe. Use lights made for outdoors outside. Do not run everything off one power board, and switch them off before bed.

Tealights in jars

Put two fingers of sand or rice in a clean glass jar and sit a tealight on top. A row of them along a wall or a windowsill is cheap and looks good.

Keep it safe. The glass gets hot. Let the jars cool before you move them.

A flower ring round the lamp

Lay rings of marigold and rose petals around a single large diya on a thali or on the floor. It takes ten minutes and needs no drawing skill.

Keep it safe. Leave a clear finger of space between the petals and the flame. Dry petals burn.

Dress the puja corner

Hang a bright dupatta or sari as a backdrop, set the idols on a raised stool covered in red cloth, and put flowers and the brass out in front.

Keep it safe. Cloth must hang well behind and above the lamps, never over them. Do not leave the puja lamp burning in an empty room.

Polish the brass

Clean the lamps, lotas and thalis with lemon and salt, rinse and dry them. Set them out where the light catches them.

Keep it safe. A metal lamp stays hot long after the flame is out. Move it by the base, with a cloth.

Paint diyas with the children

Plain clay diyas, acrylic paint and an old newspaper, a day ahead so they dry. Each child lights their own on the night.

Keep it safe. Paint the outside only and let it dry fully. An adult does the lighting.

A lamp in every dark corner

One at the gate, one at the kitchen door, one by the tulsi plant and one in each room, so no corner of the house is dark on Diwali night.

Keep it safe. Count the lamps as you light them and count them again as you put them out.

If you are a guest

Invited to a Diwali for the first time? You are welcome at all of it, the puja included.

What to say
Happy Diwali works everywhere. Shubh Deepavali means the same in Hindi. On the fourth day, Gujarati friends say Saal Mubarak, happy new year.
What to bring
A box of mithai (Indian sweets), or dried fruit and nuts.
What to wear
Something bright. Indian clothes if you have them, though nobody expects it. Many families skip black on the day.
What to do
Shoes off at the door. Join the aarti when the thali comes to you, take the prasad with your right hand, and eat more than you planned to.

Dates and the Sydney puja time are from Drik Panchang. Customs differ by region and by family. If in doubt, ask the oldest person in the room.

How a round goes

  • BootEveryone puts 1 ladoo in the pot and gets three cards, face down.
  • BlindYou bet without looking. A blind bet costs the stake, or twice the stake to raise.
  • SeenOnce you look at your cards, your bet is called a chaal. It costs twice the stake, or four times to raise.
  • PackYou fold. What you put in stays in the pot.
  • ShowWhen two players are left, either can pay for a show. The better hand takes the pot. If the hands are equal, the one who asked loses.

The stake stops at 8 and the pot at 64. Once the pot is full, everyone still in shows their cards. Dadi plays safe, Chachu bluffs and Mausi is somewhere in between.

Hands, best to worst

  • TrailThree of the same rank. Three aces beat everything.
  • Pure sequenceThree in a row, all one suit.
  • SequenceThree in a row, mixed suits.
  • ColourAll one suit, not in a row.
  • PairTwo of the same rank.
  • High cardNone of the above. The highest card decides.

A-K-Q is the highest sequence and A-2-3 comes next. In Muflis the order is reversed, so the worst hand wins. In AK47 every ace, king, four and seven is a joker. House rules differ from family to family, so agree yours before real ladoos are involved.

One card is turned up in the middle. Bet on Andar (inside) or Bahar (outside). Cards go to each side in turn, Andar first, until one matches the rank of the middle card. That side wins.

    A Diwali dinner for six

    On the table: puri, chole, aloo ki sabzi, jeera rice and cucumber raita, then kheer or gulab jamun.

    Two or three days before: namak para, mathri, shakkarpara, chivda and besan ladoo. They keep in a jar.

    The day before: kaju katli, gulab jamun and the chole.

    On the day: halwa for the puja, then the sabzi, rice and raita. Fry the puris last, as everyone sits down.

    सूजी का हलवाSooji halwaThe prasad for the puja. Serves 6, 20 minutes.

    You need

    • 1/2 cup (115 g) ghee
    • 1 cup (170 g) fine semolina (sooji)
    • 3/4 cup (165 g) sugar
    • 2 1/2 cups (625 ml) water
    • 1/4 teaspoon ground cardamom
    • 2 tablespoons cashews and raisins

    Method

    1. Bring the water and sugar to a boil in a small pan, then keep it hot.
    2. Melt the ghee in a heavy pan on low heat. Add the semolina and stir for 8 to 10 minutes, until it is golden and smells nutty.
    3. Add the cashews and raisins for the last minute.
    4. Stand back and pour in the hot syrup. It will spit. Stir for 3 to 4 minutes, until the halwa thickens and pulls away from the pan.

    रवा लड्डूRava ladooSemolina and coconut. Makes about 12, 30 minutes.

    You need

    • 1 cup (170 g) fine semolina (rava)
    • 3 tablespoons ghee
    • 1/2 cup (40 g) desiccated coconut
    • 3/4 cup (90 g) icing sugar
    • 1/4 teaspoon ground cardamom
    • 2 tablespoons chopped cashews
    • 1 tablespoon raisins
    • 3 to 4 tablespoons warm milk

    Method

    1. Roast the semolina in 2 tablespoons of the ghee on low heat for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring, until it smells nutty. Do not let it brown.

    काजू कतलीKaju katliCashew diamonds. Makes about 20, 30 minutes.

    You need

    • 1 cup (150 g) raw cashews, at room temperature
    • 1/2 cup (110 g) sugar
    • 1/4 cup (60 ml) water
    • 1 teaspoon ghee

    Method

    1. Grind the cashews to a fine powder in short bursts. Stop before the powder turns oily. Sieve it and grind any coarse bits again.
    2. Stir the sugar and water in a non-stick pan on medium heat until the sugar dissolves, then boil for 1 minute.
    3. Turn the heat to low, add the cashew powder and stir for 5 to 7 minutes, until it comes together as one soft lump that leaves the pan.
    4. Test it: a small piece should roll into a ball that does not stick to your fingers.
    5. Tip onto baking paper, add the ghee and knead for a minute while it is still warm, until smooth.
    6. Roll out between two sheets of paper to 5 mm thick. Cut into diamonds once cool.
    गुलाब जामुनGulab jamunMade with milk powder. Makes about 16, 45 minutes plus 2 hours to soak.

    You need

    • For the syrup: 1 1/2 cups (330 g) sugar
    • 1 1/2 cups (375 ml) water
    • 4 cardamom pods, crushed
    • 1 teaspoon rose water
    • For the dough: 1 cup (120 g) full-cream milk powder
    • 1/4 cup (30 g) plain flour
    • 1/4 teaspoon bicarbonate of soda
    • 1 tablespoon ghee, melted
    • 4 to 5 tablespoons milk
    • Oil for deep-frying

    Method

    1. Simmer the sugar, water and cardamom for 5 minutes, until the syrup feels slightly sticky. Add the rose water and keep it warm.
    2. Mix the milk powder, flour and soda. Rub in the ghee, then add the milk a tablespoon at a time to make a soft, slightly sticky dough. Do not knead it.
    3. Rest the dough for 5 minutes. With greased hands, roll it into 16 smooth balls with no cracks.
    4. Heat the oil on low. A small piece of dough should sink, then rise slowly. If it rises at once, the oil is too hot.
    5. Fry in two batches for 6 to 8 minutes, turning all the time, until deep brown all over.
    6. Drain for a minute, then drop them into the warm syrup. Soak for at least 2 hours.
    शक्करपाराShakkarparaSweet fried diamonds. Makes a large jar, 45 minutes.

    नमक पारेNamak paraCrisp salted diamonds. Makes a large jar, 45 minutes.

    • 1/4 cup (60 ml) oil or melted ghee

    Method

    1. Mix the flour, ajwain and salt. Rub in the oil until it looks like breadcrumbs.

    पोहा चिवड़ाPoha chivdaThe savoury mix. Makes about 5 cups, 20 minutes.

    You need

    • 3 cups (150 g) thin poha (flattened rice)
    • 3 tablespoons oil
    • 1/2 cup (75 g) peanuts
    • 1/4 cup cashews
    • 1/4 cup roasted chana dal
    • 2 tablespoons raisins
    • 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
    • 12 curry leaves
    • 2 green chillies, sliced
    • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
    • A pinch of asafoetida (hing)
    • 1 teaspoon icing sugar
    • 3/4 teaspoon salt

    Method

    1. Dry roast the poha in a wide pan on low heat for 5 to 6 minutes, stirring gently, until it is crisp and snaps when pressed. Tip into a bowl.
    2. Heat the oil in the same pan. Fry the peanuts until golden, then the cashews, chana dal and raisins. Lift out and add to the poha.
    3. Add the mustard seeds to the oil left in the pan. When they pop, add the curry leaves and chillies and fry until crisp, then the turmeric and asafoetida.
    4. Turn off the heat, return everything to the pan and toss well with the sugar and salt.
    5. Cool completely before storing.
    मठरीMathriFlaky salted biscuits. Makes about 25, 50 minutes.

    You need

    • 2 cups (250 g) plain flour
    • 1/4 cup (40 g) fine semolina
    • 1 teaspoon carom seeds (ajwain)
    • 1 teaspoon coarsely crushed black pepper
    • 1 teaspoon salt
    • 4 tablespoons ghee
    • About 1/2 cup (125 ml) water
    • Oil for deep-frying

    Method

    1. Mix the flour, semolina, carom seeds, pepper and salt.
    2. Rub in the ghee until the mix looks like breadcrumbs and holds its shape when you squeeze a handful.
